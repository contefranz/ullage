Reading notes:
- 30 590 and 30 596: the middle "9" of 590 and the final "6" of 596 are faintly printed (broken strokes)
  but legible; I read them as 9 and 6 with good confidence (590 also fits the monotonic sequence).
- "Drogue deployment" event label sits on the 0.42 row (first row of the last block).
Arithmetic checks:
- weight/mass ratio is 32.20 +/- 0.01 for every row EXCEPT the first: 11 959 / 369.7 = 32.35.
  The table therefore converts slugs->lb with g ~ 32.2, not with the 32.1740 ft/s^2 given in the
  symbols list (e.g. 362.1 x 32.174 = 11 650, table says 11 659).
- SUSPECTED MISPRINT, row 1 (Entry): mass 369.7 slugs is inconsistent with 11 959 lb.
  11 959 / 32.2 = 371.4 slugs; 369.7 x 32.2 = 11 904 lb.
  The weight 11 959 looks right: 11 959 - 11 659 = 300 lb = 90 lb RCS propellant + 210 lb ablator
  (text p16-17), and 11 959 - 11 857 = 102 lb entry->first peak g matches 371.4 - 368.2 = 3.2 slugs
  (= 103 lb), whereas 369.7 - 368.2 = 1.5 slugs (= 48 lb). So the mass 369.7 is probably a
  misprint for ~371.4 (or the mass column was computed from a different entry weight).
- Mach is not monotonic (25.6 at entry interface, 29.2 at first peak g): plausible because of the
  high temperature (speed of sound) at 400 000 ft, but note the conclusions (p28) quote initial-entry
  Mach "approximately 38", which does not match 25.6 / 29.2 here.
- X increases monotonically 1039.58 -> 1039.79; Z decreases 6.57 -> 6.27 (with Y offset 0.3 in
  included, per footnote c).

p3 [printed ii]  ABSTRACT
=====================================================================
- "Apollo 4 entry configuration (Command Module 017)"
- modified trim-wind-tunnel data vs flight: initial trim angle of attack lower by approximately 1.5 deg
- resultant lift-to-drag ratio higher by approximately 10 percent
- flight-derived L/D and angles of attack essentially linear with Mach number down to 6.0, at which point
  comparison showed much better agreement.

- d   spacecraft reference diameter, 12.833 ft
- g   acceleration of gravity at the surface of the earth, 32.1740 ft/sec^2
- S   reference area, 129.35 ft^2                      (p12)
- m   spacecraft mass, slugs; p_t, p_inf in psia; q in lb/ft^2; V in ft/sec; angles in deg
- C_A = -m A_x / (q_inf S); C_N = -m A_z / (q_inf S); C_Y = m A_y / (q_inf S)
- C_L,R = sqrt(C_L,H^2 + C_L,V^2); C_N,R = sqrt(C_Y^2 + C_N^2)
- (L/D)_H = C_L,H/C_D; (L/D)_R = C_L,R/C_D; (L/D)_V = C_L,V/C_D
- t: elapsed time from range zero = first integral second of range time prior to Saturn
  instrument-unit umbilical disconnect, sec
- L_f deceleration load factor, printed as sqrt(A_x^2 + A_y^2 + A_x^2)/g
  MISPRINT: the third term is printed A_x (checked at 300%), should be A_z.
Checks:
- pi/4 x 12.833^2 = 129.344 ft^2 (would round to 129.34); pi/4 x (154 in/12)^2 = 129.350 ft^2.
  So S = 129.35 corresponds to d = 154 in = 12.8333 ft exactly; 12.833 is that value truncated.
  Not an error, just rounding.

p28 [printed 20]  end of results + CONCLUSIONS
=====================================================================
- (carry-over) "... 25 percent) to good (within 50 percent) agreement down to M_inf = 1.35."
- C_Y, C_N in figures 19(c), 19(d); sideslip beta fig. 14(b)
- Conclusion 1: at initial-entry point, flight-derived steady-state resultant trim angle of attack
  approximately 154.5 deg; average resultant L/D approximately 0.368 (modified trim-wind-tunnel data
  1.5 deg lower and 10 percent higher, respectively). Trend essentially linear with Mach, reaching
  flight-derived trim resultant alpha approximately 153 deg and L/D approximately 0.410 at Mach 6.0
  (modified trim-wind-tunnel values 0.5 deg higher and 3 percent lower, respectively). Good agreement
  from Mach 4.0 down to Mach 1.35 and below Mach 0.9, with deviations in trim resultant alpha up to
  10 deg and in trim resultant L/D up to 25 percent in the transonic region.
- Conclusion 2: hybrid data agreed with flight-derived data down to Mach 1.2. Previous flight AS-202.
  Modified wind-tunnel force data reliable even at the initial-entry high-Mach-number (approx 38),
  low-Reynolds-number (approx 1.0 x 10^4) conditions; trim attitudes (moment data) poor in the
  initial-entry hypersonic region (Mach greater than 18) and possibly in the transonic region (Mach
  greater than 0.9 and less than 1.20).
- Conclusion 3: force-coefficient data based on flight-measured stagnation pressure reliable down to
  Mach 4.0.
Notes:
- Abstract says modified trim-wind-tunnel initial trim alpha "lower by approximately 1.5 deg" and L/D
  "higher by approximately 10 percent": consistent with conclusion 1.
- Mach "approx 38" at initial entry vs Table II Mach 25.6 at entry interface / 29.2 at first peak g:
  inconsistent as printed (possibly a different Mach definition / atmosphere, but flag it).
- Implied wind-tunnel values: alpha_R 153.0 deg and L/D 0.405 at entry (154.5-1.5, 0.368x1.10);
  153.5 deg and 0.398 at Mach 6 (153+0.5, 0.410x0.97).
