p14 [printed 8]  Main-Parachute-Cluster Interference
=====================================================================
  - November 27, 1962: first aerial drop test, parachute test vehicle (PTV), cluster of three
    independently deployed 88.1-foot-diameter ringsail main parachutes
  - vehicle ballasted to only 4750 pounds, one-half the weight of the CM (=> CM 9500 lb, matching the
    Fig. 5 level 9 500 in 1963-64)
  - removal of 75 percent of the material from the fifth ring of the canopy (open ring)
  - midgore reefing (reefing rings moved to the middle of the gore on the skirt band)
  - remove four complete gores -> basic 68-gore-configuration main parachute
  - net allowable weight saving approximately 45 pounds in main parachute and harness assembly
  - specified maximum rate of descent of 33 fps at 5000-foot pressure altitude
  - two-parachute cluster oscillations reduced from approximately +/-20 deg to +/-6 deg
Note: Fig. 5 places the "first ELS aerial drop test" triangle a little before the 1963 tick (my reading
  ~1962.6-1962.7), text says November 27, 1962 (=1962.9); the marker position is approximate, likely
  not a real conflict.

p51 [printed C-3]  Apollo 15 MAIN PARACHUTE FAILURE / DESCRIPTION AND SYSTEM OPERATION
=====================================================================
  - Apollo 15: three mains deployed and inflated properly at approximately 10 000 feet altitude
  - obscured by clouds at about 7000 feet; emerging at about 6000 feet one main was deflated (figure 1)
  - one of the six riser links had a broken stud and three others had cracks
  - landing sequence initiated at a nominal altitude of 24 000 feet with forward heat shield jettison
  - 7.2-foot-diameter parachute mortar-deployed from the forward heat shield
  - two 16.5-foot diameter conical ribbon-type drogues mortar-deployed 1.6 seconds after FHS jettison;
    10 seconds later inflate to fully open; released at about 11 000 feet
  - three 7.2-foot diameter ring-slot pilot parachutes at drogue disconnect
  - each main inflates through two reefing stages; main parachute assemblies in fig. 2
  - each main canopy consists of twelve rings of sails, each ring divided into 68 gores; canopy
    terminates in 68 suspension (lines; sentence continues on next page)
Note: "approximately 10 000 feet" for Apollo 15 main inflation vs nominal 11 000 ft drogue release
  elsewhere: consistent (inflation follows release by a few seconds).
